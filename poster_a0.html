From Campus to Code: How I Learn Languages & Programming with Iterating AI
Exploring Human-AI Co-learning in University Self-Study

1Introduction & My AI-Driven Learning Practice

1.1  AI as My Personal University Learning Assistant

As an undergraduate student, I integrate large-model AI tools into my daily self-study for two core subjects: computer programming and English language learning. Unlike traditional learning resources, modern generative AI can respond instantly to open-ended questions, provide custom-tailored examples, and give iterative feedback for my code drafts and written work. Nevertheless, AI is not an infallible tutor. Model hallucinations, logical mistakes in code output, and grammatically unnatural English expressions appear frequently, which means I must constantly evaluate and verify every piece of information generated by AI. This poster describes my practical learning workflow, tracks the technical evolution of large language models, and reflects on both the opportunities and hidden risks of AI-augmented university-level learning.

1.2  My Practical Uses for AI in Programming Learning

I rely on AI throughout my programming practice process, but I never copy-paste finished code directly. When I encounter runtime errors or confusing algorithm logic, I describe my problem and my own attempted solutions to the AI. I ask it to explain error tracebacks line-by-line, break down abstract concepts such as loops, recursion and object-oriented programming, and produce minimal demonstrative example snippets. After receiving these materials, I close the AI window and attempt to rewrite the program independently. If my new version still fails, I compare my work against AI-provided examples to locate gaps in my logic. This method helps me overcome the difficulty of limited one-on-one teacher consultation time outside class hours.

1.3  My Practical Uses for AI in English Language & Writing Practice

For my English improvement, AI supports multiple practice dimensions. I use it to simulate real-time oral conversation scenarios for daily dialogue and academic discussion topics. For written assignments, I submit my original full draft first instead of asking AI to write for me. I request feedback on article structure, logical coherence, word choice, grammatical errors and sentence variety. I will compare the revised version with my original writing, note down recurring grammar mistakes, and rewrite the whole composition on my own. I also feed complex academic paragraphs to AI for simplified paraphrasing and detailed grammar parsing, helping me process difficult reading materials more efficiently.

2My Complete AI-Assisted Learning Workflow

2.1  Four-Stage Learning Cycle

This structured four-stage cycle prevents over-reliance and keeps human critical thinking at the centre of learning.

1

Question Formulation & Clear Prompt Design

Before consulting AI, I define concrete learning objectives. I record my existing knowledge, my attempted solutions and my specific confusion. Detailed, context-rich prompts produce far more useful replies than vague requests. I avoid commands such as “solve this for me”. Instead, I describe what I already know and what part I want to understand.

Keywords: set clear goals; state prior thinking; avoid direct-solution prompts
2

Receive Explanation & Independent Practice

I read through AI explanations and sample outputs carefully. Then I separate myself from AI-generated content and carry out hands-on practice: writing full program scripts or completing English essays and speaking tasks entirely by my own effort. This independent practice step is non-negotiable; it transfers external AI knowledge into my personal long-term understanding.

Keywords: knowledge absorption; hands-on practice; knowledge internalisation
3

Comparative Checking & Error-spotting

Once I finish my own work, I use AI as a reviewing tool. For programming, I compare my code logic against AI examples, identify bugs and test program runtime behaviour. For English writing, I check grammar, cohesion and vocabulary selection. Importantly, I do not treat AI feedback as absolute truth. I actively look for possible AI mistakes and questionable reasoning.

Keywords: cross-comparison; error detection; challenge AI outputs
4

Knowledge Summarisation & Critical Reflection

I compile my mistakes, key concepts and takeaways into personal study notes. I evaluate whether the AI answer is logically consistent and factually correct. I think over what I have mastered and what knowledge points still require further review. This final stage consolidates learning and strengthens my independent judgement capacity.

Keywords: note-taking; critical evaluation; knowledge consolidation

3Opportunities, Risks & Challenges

3.1  Benefits & Opportunities

  • ✓On-demand tutoring: learning support available at any time beyond classroom hours
  • ✓Low-cost repeated practice: unlimited simulated coding trials and foreign-language conversation scenarios
  • ✓Personalised feedback: AI adapts exercises to my current skill level
  • ✓Lower barrier to entry: complex programming and academic English become easier to approach for self-learners

3.2  Risks & Key Challenges

  • !AI hallucination: plausible-sounding yet wrong code, grammar rules and factual explanations
  • !Over-dependence: letting AI generate full assignments eliminates independent thinking
  • !Hidden bias in training data may skew language outputs and coding suggestions
  • !Judgement gap: students may lack experience judging AI-produced content

3.3  Problems This Approach Helps Solve

Many university students face insufficient one-to-one teaching resources. AI fills gaps for after-class practice, gives instant feedback and helps learners tackle difficult topics without waiting for teacher office hours. Still, AI serves only as a supplementary tool rather than a replacement for human-led learning.

4My Perspective & Personal Reflection (20% of assignment grade)

I selected this topic because AI-assisted study forms a large part of my university daily life. I am fascinated by how fast large-model technology evolves, and I want to build healthy, effective learning habits before AI tools become even more powerful in the future. Working on this poster helped me formalise my scattered learning habits into a clear repeatable workflow. I realised that technical progress of AI does not automatically make people better learners. The value comes from how human learners organise questions, complete independent practice, and maintain critical evaluation towards machine outputs. Even with highly-advanced future-generation models, human-initiated thinking, practice and reflection will stay irreplaceable for mastering programming and language ability.

CORE TAKEAWAY

Evolving AI is a powerful learning tool, yet critical human thinking remains irreplaceable in language and programming study.
